About

A Life Dedicated to Science & Nation

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born in a humble Tamil Muslim family in Rameswaram. Rising from modest beginnings, he became one of India's greatest aerospace scientists and a beloved President who connected with every citizen — especially the youth.

As the chief architect of India's missile program, he led the development of Agni and Prithvi missiles. His work at ISRO and DRDO earned him the title "Missile Man of India" and the nation's highest civilian honor, the Bharat Ratna.

Beyond laboratories and launch pads, Kalam was a teacher at heart. He visited hundreds of schools and universities, inspiring students to dream big and work hard for a developed India by 2020.

Timeline

A Journey Through Time

Key milestones that shaped a legend and a nation.

1931

Born in Rameswaram

Born on October 15 in a Tamil Muslim family. Sold newspapers to support his education while nurturing a passion for flight and science.

1954

Graduated from MIT

Graduated in Aeronautical Engineering from Madras Institute of Technology, beginning his remarkable career in aerospace.

1969

Joined ISRO

Transferred to ISRO where he served as project director of SLV-III, India's first indigenous satellite launch vehicle.

1983

Missile Program Leader

Appointed chief of DRDO's Integrated Guided Missile Development Program, leading Agni and Prithvi missile projects.

2002

President of India

Elected as the 11th President of India. Known as the "People's President" for his accessibility and connection with citizens.

2015

Eternal Legacy

Passed away on July 27 while delivering a lecture at IIM Shillong. His legacy continues to inspire generations worldwide.
